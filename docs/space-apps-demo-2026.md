# AstroBone: three-minute challenge demonstration

Challenge: [Create Health Monitoring Software for Astronauts on Space Missions](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/health-monitoring-software/).

The core story is **Gather health indicators → Evaluate usable changes → Act on a clear next step**. Breathing capture is an optional experimental addition.

1. **0:00–0:25 — The mission.** Open Mission Control. Explain why crews must assess themselves when ground support is delayed. Point to the original logo and the explicit Elena synthetic-mission label.
2. **0:25–1:20 — Gather.** Use the main **Open daily self-check** button. For a dependable presentation, choose **Elena · synthetic demo** and state that its readings are fictional. Show the safety check, movement-quality refusal/repeat, reaction-test demo, sleep and mood, and body readings. Explain that cuffs, glucose meters and thermometers supply typed readings; the webcam does not measure those quantities. Show **You · this device** as the separate personal path, with optional local saving.
3. **1:20–1:55 — Evaluate and act.** Show a result with its usable trend, source, quality and reason for attention. Open the next action, due time and flight-surgeon handoff. Change the mission environment to explain delayed support. Point out routine timing and badges rewarding completion and repeat quality, not healthier numbers.
4. **1:55–2:35 — Follow the evidence.** Return to Mission Control and open NASA evidence. Show the original NASA files, checksums and population labels. The two new microCT tables add 62 source rows; OSD-489 shares the RR-1 mission with existing OSD-804 and is not an independent replication. Connect a finding to the relevant check, then show clickable anatomy and its function/effects explanation.
5. **2:35–3:00 — Optional respiratory research.** Open the secondary **Breathing check** entry and preview the explicitly synthetic waveform. Show its quality diagnostics, personal-history separation and pending belt-validation status. Describe measured RGB-D imports honestly: direct depth-camera hardware and dense depth heatmaps are not included.

NASA’s [2026 announcement](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/nasa-welcomes-all-ages-experience-levels-for-space-apps-challenge/) lists the November 14–15 event dates. That source was checked November 1, 2026. This project is independent; using NASA data does not imply NASA endorsement or an award.

The strongest claims are testable: working health-input and review flows, source-derived NASA values, clear quality refusals, reproducible provenance, and a written validation plan. No astronaut study or respiratory-belt accuracy evaluation has been completed. The app gives bounded, human-reviewed next steps and points to approved mission procedures; it does not prescribe treatment.
